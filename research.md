# Research Overview

## Purpose of the Project
In Vero Vacuo investigates Higgs‑sector vacuum structure and metastability using
modern semiclassical and lattice‑based computational methods. Traditional
Coleman–Callan O(4) symmetry assumptions fail in many physically relevant
scenarios. This project develops tools that remain valid when symmetry is
broken, backgrounds are nontrivial, or multiple fields interact.

Our goal is to build a reliable, extensible framework for computing decay‑rate
prefactors in settings where analytic methods are insufficient.

---

## Why This Work Matters
Vacuum decay plays a central role in early‑universe cosmology, electroweak
stability, and phase transitions in gauge theories. However, the fluctuation
determinants that control decay‑rate prefactors are notoriously difficult to
compute outside idealized symmetric cases.

This project addresses that gap by developing:

- **Validated SU(2) lattice machinery** for semiclassical decay
- **Stochastic‑Lanczos trace‑log evaluation** for large operators
- **Nonlinear vertex construction** for multi‑field potentials
- **Brillouin‑zone matching** for continuum comparison
- **MS‑scheme cross‑checks** for renormalization consistency
- **Spacing extrapolation** to ensure continuum‑limit reliability

These methods allow us to explore metastability in realistic models rather than
toy approximations.

---

## Current Directions
### 1. Multi‑Field Vacuum Decay
Developing tools for potentials with multiple interacting scalar fields,
including cases with saddle‑point structure and nontrivial tunneling paths.

### 2. Finite‑Temperature Backgrounds
Extending fluctuation‑operator construction to thermal ensembles and
temperature‑dependent effective potentials.

### 3. Gauge‑Theory Structure
Building SU(2) lattice operators with correct nonlinear vertices and validating
their behavior against continuum expectations.

### 4. Determinant Evaluation
Implementing stochastic‑Lanczos trace‑log methods to compute fluctuation
determinants efficiently for large sparse operators.

### 5. Continuum Comparison
Using Brillouin‑zone matching and MS‑scheme cross‑checks to ensure that lattice
results agree with continuum predictions.

---

## Long‑Term Vision
The long‑term aim is to establish a robust computational framework for
metastability studies across a wide range of physical settings:

- Higgs‑sector vacuum decay  
- Early‑universe phase transitions  
- Curved‑spacetime backgrounds  
- Gauge theories with nontrivial structure  

This project seeks to make nonperturbative semiclassical calculations accessible
to researchers working in cosmology, particle physics, and quantum field theory.
